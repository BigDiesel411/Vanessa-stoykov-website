Resilience Is the New Black

By Vanessa Stoykov

Every time I sit down to write about money, something seems to shift. The cost of living, work, family responsibilities and events around the world can all change quickly. It’s a reminder that uncertainty isn’t an occasional interruption to life. It’s part of life.

What matters is how prepared we feel to meet it.

Resilience doesn’t mean never worrying or being able to absorb every setback without help. It means finding a way through: adapting when plans change, asking for support and taking the next step, even when you don’t yet know exactly where it will lead.

We show resilience in all kinds of ways. Getting through a difficult period at work. Recovering after a relationship ends. Facing an unexpected bill. Helping someone we love through a hard time. Often, we only recognise how strong we’ve been when we stop and look back.

Money is part of that resilience. The unexpected can put pressure on our finances, but practical steps can give us more options when life changes. You might start by rebuilding savings, even in small amounts. Check whether your insurance still suits your needs. Make or update your will. Talk with your family about plans for the future. If you’re unsure what to do, prepare questions for a qualified professional.

These steps may not feel urgent today. But at a Financial Flashpoint, a moment when a major change in life brings important money decisions with it, having a plan can help you think more clearly about what matters and what to do next.

You don’t need to solve everything at once. Choose one thing you can do this week. Review a bill. Set up a small automatic transfer to savings. Book a conversation you’ve been putting off. One step can make the next one easier.

Resilience is also about people. The support we give and receive, the conversations we have and the connections we make can help us face uncertainty with greater confidence. Think about what you’ve already managed, and the people who have helped you along the way. Acknowledge that strength, and consider who might need your support now.

We can’t know what will come next. But we can strengthen our financial foundations, stay connected and give ourselves room to make thoughtful decisions when life changes.

Start with one conversation. Make sure you feel comfortable with your plan B.
