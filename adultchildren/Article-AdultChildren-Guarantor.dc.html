Should You Be a Guarantor for Your Adult Child's Home Loan?

By Vanessa Stoykov

When an adult child asks you to guarantee their first home loan, you're not just facing a paperwork decision, you're staring down one of the biggest money conversations in modern family life. As property prices soar and lending criteria tighten in Australia, the pressure on parents to help out is real. But before you sign anything, it's crucial to understand exactly what you're taking on; and how it could impact your own retirement planning.

Understand the Real Risks of Being a Guarantor

Agreeing to be a guarantor for your child's mortgage isn't just a kind gesture, it's a binding financial commitment. If your child can't meet their repayments, the bank can demand the guaranteed amount from you. This could put your own home or retirement savings at risk, especially if you're close to retiring and have fewer years to recover financially.

You might trust your child completely, but life is unpredictable. Job loss, illness, relationship breakdown, or rising interest rates can happen to anyone. If disaster strikes, being a guarantor can quickly turn from supportive to stressful.

Ask the Right Questions Before You Decide

It's normal to feel torn between wanting to help your child and needing to protect your own future. Start by having a frank, honest conversation as a family. Ask questions like:

  • What happens if your income changes or you lose your job?
  • How long do you realistically expect the guarantee to stay in place?
  • What is the plan for refinancing down the track?
  • Could buying a less expensive property remove the need for a guarantor?

Having these conversations early helps set clear expectations and avoids future conflict. Protecting your own retirement is not selfish, it's smart.

Get Independent Legal and Financial Advice

Before signing any documents, it's essential to get advice from a professional who isn't emotionally involved. Legal advisers can explain exactly what you're liable for and help identify any potential risks in the loan agreement. Financial advisers can model your retirement plans and show how acting as a guarantor might affect your ability to retire on time or borrow in the future.

Consider the Long-Term Impact on Your Retirement

Many parents underestimate how much financial security matters in the later stages of life. If acting as a guarantor puts your home or savings at risk, you could end up needing financial support from your children down the track. No one wants that cycle of dependency.

Sometimes, the greatest gift you can give your children is to remain financially independent. That way, you're not only supporting them today, you're making sure you won't become a burden tomorrow.

The Bottom Line: Make a Decision You'll Be Proud Of

There's no shame in saying no if the risks are too high. Open, courageous conversations about money protect everyone involved. If you do decide to help, make sure you set boundaries, put everything in writing, and review the arrangement regularly. And if you choose not to, explain your reasons with honesty and compassion.

Your financial wellbeing matters, now, and in the years to come. The goal isn't just to help your child buy a home. It's to make a decision you'll thank yourself for later.
